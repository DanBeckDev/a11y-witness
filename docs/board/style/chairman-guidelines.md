# The chairman's conditions for any document presented to the board

Given 2026-09-06, and binding on every edition from that day. Recorded here beside the other two guides
so an edition is checked against the text rather than against anyone's memory of it.

1. **Think first, then prompt.** The message, the audience and the outcome are stated before anything is
   generated.
2. **Read every generated word and number before sending.** The sender is responsible for all of it and
   must be able to defend every sentence and its tone.
3. **Check the length.** Anything over one page carries a succinct **executive summary**, written by
   hand — never left for the reader's own AI to produce.
4. **Check the voice.** First person must sound like the sender; otherwise revise it, or write in the
   third person.

**A receiver is entitled to say "tighten this up" before reading.**

## How this document meets them

**The summary is written by hand, per edition, and its absence stops the edition.**
`docs/board/summaries/<date>.md` holds at most 120 words answering three questions: are we on the date,
what changed since yesterday, and what the board must decide today. The generator refuses to render or
post without one dated for that day, and the scheduled job records the refusal in its log. **A missing
summary is a missing edition, never a summary-less document** — a summary assembled from the sections
below it would be exactly the machine-written summary rule 3 forbids.

**The body is capped and the cap is enforced on the rendered output.** Sections one to five fit two
pages; the appendix is separate and may be skipped. Edition 2 ran to 1,864 words in the body and four
pages, which is why the cap exists and why the guard is shown to fail against it. **Cut repetition and
evidence-in-prose, never a decision or a number** — evidence belongs in the appendix, and a number that
makes a claim actionable is never dropped or rounded.

**One voice: the project speaking as "we", presented by the chief executive.** No sentence goes in that
the chairman could not say aloud to the board in their own voice. The guard checks shape; a person reads
the sentences, and says in the handover what they read and what they changed — so the chief executive's
read is a second read rather than the first.
