Given by the chairman on 2026-09-06 as the final condition before any document is presented to the board
("NBL AI Content Generation Guidelines", for content meant for internal human consumption).

**The four author rules:**
1. Think first, then prompt: the message, the audience, the outcome, in my own words, before anything is generated.
2. Read every generated word and number before sending. I am responsible for all of it; if I would not defend a sentence or its tone, revise it.
3. Check the length. Anything over one page carries a succinct executive summary written by hand, never left for the reader's own AI to produce.
4. Check the voice. First-person content must sound like the sender; otherwise revise or write in the third person.

**Why:** AI content fatigue: long, redundant, over-detailed documents waste the board's time and erode trust. Edition 2 of the board report was six pages for a daily.

**How to apply:**
- I read every page of a board document before it goes (I did for editions 1 and 2, and caught a number disagreeing with itself); keep doing it, and say in the handover what I read.
- The board document opens with an executive summary the product manager writes by hand for that edition; the 08:00 job refuses to post without one dated for the day.
- Body target: two pages plus appendix; a rendered-document test caps body length.
- Voice: the document speaks as the project in the first person plural, consistently; it is presented by `ceo`.
- Receivers may ask for a tighter draft; a "tighten this" from the board is a normal response, not a failure.
